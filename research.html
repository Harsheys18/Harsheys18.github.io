Robotics & Embodied AI

Sakhi — AI Companion Robot

Technical Lead & Founding Member · IDEAS Program, IIT Bombay · 2025–current

Sakhi is an attempt to build a companion robot that behaves like one coherent agent rather than a set of independently built modules stitched together. As technical lead, I work on the perception-to-dialogue pipeline that ties speech-to-text, facial emotion detection, LLM-based reasoning, and expressive text-to-speech into a single real-time loop, so the robot can hold a conversation while staying responsive to who it's talking to and how they're reacting. The project was selected for IDEAS Level 1, has since advanced to Level 2, and has secured funding to continue development and validation.

Perception and RL-Based Robot Control

B.Tech Project · Advisor: Prof. Nived Chebrolu, IIT Bombay · Jul '26–current

For my B.Tech project, I'm looking at how a robot's control policy can be built directly on learned visual representations rather than hand-designed state estimates, aiming for decision-making that stays robust when observations are incomplete or noisy. Alongside this, I'm exploring neuro-symbolic and hierarchical approaches that let structured task constraints shape the learned policy over long horizons, and studying closed-loop setups where perception and control adapt to each other instead of being trained and deployed separately.

Safe Reinforcement Learning

Safety in Reinforcement Learning

Research Internship, Ong's Group · Advisor: Prof. Luke Ong, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore · May–Jul '25

This internship examined how standard reinforcement-learning algorithms hold up once safety constraints enter the picture — specifically, how policy-gradient methods like PPO and TRPO can be adapted to incorporate feedback from LIDAR and hazard heuristics in continuous-control settings. I worked within Safety Gymnasium, a model-free benchmark with 60-dimensional state spaces, and spent time understanding where existing safe-RL approaches break down, including how they compare against ideas drawn from control theory.

Formal Verification

Neural Closure Certificates for Verification

R&D Course Project · Advisor: Prof. Akshay S, IIT Bombay · Aug–Nov '25

This project asked whether a neural network could be trained not just to control a system, but to certify that the system stays safe. I formulated template-based closure certificates and trained networks to learn valid certificate functions, then evaluated the resulting safety guarantees on benchmark dynamical systems, including a two-room temperature model — essentially testing whether a learned certificate can offer the kind of guarantee usually reserved for a hand-derived proof.

Timed Automaton and Its Applications

R&D Course Project · Advisor: Prof. Krishna Shankar Narayan, IIT Bombay · Jan–Apr '25

A more theoretical detour into formal methods: studying reachability and closure properties of timed automata through region and zone graph constructions, and comparing decidability across variants such as event-clock, updatable-timed, and one-clock automata.

Machine Learning Systems & Quantitative Research

Double XGBoost: Depth-Wise Boosting

Software Engineer, Juspay · Research problem statement, Mumbai · Dec '25–Jan '26 · work in progress toward publication

This started as an open research question at Juspay: whether a boosted-tree model could gain expressivity by going deeper rather than simply adding more trees. I proposed Double XGBoost, which introduces depth-wise boosting, and derived a second-order optimization formulation via Taylor expansion so that node-level predictors could be trained in closed form. Early results show faster convergence and improved accuracy from the residual-correction step; the work is still in progress toward publication.

Quantitative Research: Portfolio Optimization & Alpha Signals

Quantitative Researcher, Graviton · Gurugram, India · May–Jul '26

At Graviton, I worked on portfolio optimization for long-short strategies, focusing on systematic weight allocation, and on evaluating ML-based alpha signals across different model configurations. Part of the role involved building a reusable Python library and an interactive UI so the rest of the team could run training and backtesting experiments and compare alpha performance without rebuilding the pipeline each time.
